# PART 2 — SUPPLEMENTS

**Framing:** supplements are tier 5 of 6 in the hierarchy (`00-framework.md`). This file
is short on purpose. A user asking about supplements before they've fixed sleep, protein,
and adherence is asking the wrong question, and the coach should say so — politely, once,
and then answer the question they actually asked.

---

## The tier list

### Tier A — strong evidence, worth recommending

| Supplement | Dose | What it actually does | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | 3–5 g/day, no loading needed | Increases phosphocreatine stores → more work at high intensity → more accumulated stimulus. Also some cognitive/recovery evidence. | The most studied ergogenic aid in existence. **Safe at recommended doses with no evidence of renal harm in healthy individuals across use up to 5 years.** Monohydrate only — the exotic forms are marketing. |
| **Caffeine** | 3–6 mg/kg, 30–60 min pre | Small-to-moderate improvements in muscular endurance, strength, sprinting, jumping, throwing, movement velocity | Tolerance develops. Watch the interaction with sleep — a late dose costs more than it gives. Half-life ~5–6 h means a 2pm dose is still active at bedtime. |
| **Protein powder** | As needed to hit target | Convenience, not magic. It's food. | Only "works" insofar as it closes a gap between actual and target intake. |
| **Beta-alanine** | 4–6 g/day, ≥2–4 weeks to load | Buffers intramuscular acidosis. Most pronounced in efforts **1–4 min** | Directly relevant to rowing 2K, combat rounds, and strongman events in the training library. Paraesthesia (tingling) is the only common side effect; split doses to reduce it. |

### Tier B — context-dependent, defensible

- **Vitamin D** — if deficient (blood test, not guesswork). Common in northern latitudes and indoor athletes.
- **Omega-3 / EPA+DHA** — if dietary fish intake is low.
- **Iron** — **only if labs confirm deficiency.** Common in female endurance athletes. Supplementing iron without deficiency is harmful, not neutral.
- **Electrolytes/sodium** — for genuinely high sweat losses (long, hot, heavy sessions). Not a general daily need.
- **Nitrate/beetroot** — modest endurance benefit, mostly in sub-elite athletes at moderate intensities.

### Tier C — nearly everything else

BCAAs (redundant if total protein is adequate — which it should be), testosterone
boosters, fat burners, most pre-workout blends beyond their caffeine content, glutamine,
"detox" products, most greens powders. **The coach should not recommend these**, and
should be able to explain why without being preachy about it.

---

## Product-relevant cautions

- **Third-party testing matters** for anyone who might be drug-tested (some users will
  compete). Look for NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport. The supplement industry
  is not meaningfully pre-market regulated in the US, and contamination is documented.
- **Interactions and conditions are out of scope for this app.** Anyone on medication,
  pregnant, or with a diagnosed condition needs a real clinician, not an AI coach's
  supplement suggestion. See `04-safety-and-scope.md`.
- **Recommending supplements creates a commercial-conflict question** if the platform
  ever monetizes through affiliate links or its own product line. Worth deciding
  deliberately before it comes up, not after — flagged for the decision log, not
  resolved here.
