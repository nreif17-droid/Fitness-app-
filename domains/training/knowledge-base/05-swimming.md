# PART 5 — SWIMMING

## Why swimming inverts the usual rules

In running and cycling, fitness is the limiter. In swimming, **technique is the limiter** — drag rises with the square of velocity, so a stronger swimmer with poor position simply creates more drag more energetically. A beginner cyclist wastes maybe 10% of their output; a beginner swimmer can waste 70%. **This is the one modality where "just train harder" is actively counterproductive.**

### CSS — Critical Swim Speed (your threshold)

Swim a **400 m TT** and a **200 m TT** (fully rested between).
`CSS pace per 100 m = (400 − 200 distance) ÷ (400 time − 200 time)`
Example: 400 in 6:40 (400 s), 200 in 3:10 (190 s) → 200 ÷ 210 s = **1:45 per 100 m**. All threshold work below is prescribed off CSS.

## 5A. Beginner: Technique-First Base

**Who it's for:** can float and move but has no efficient stroke, or gets winded in 50 m.
**Goal by Week 12:** swim 1000–1500 m continuously with a repeatable freestyle stroke.
**Frequency:** 3 swims/wk. **Consistency beats duration in swimming — 3×30 min beats 1×90 min by a wide margin.**

### The five technique priorities, in order

1. **Body position** — head neutral (look down/slightly forward), hips high, press the chest slightly. Sinking legs are a head-position problem 90% of the time.
2. **Breathing** — exhale continuously underwater, turn (don't lift) the head, one goggle in the water. Holding your breath is the single biggest cause of panic and CO₂ buildup.
3. **Rotation** — rotate from the hips ~45°, swim on your side, not flat.
4. **Catch** — high elbow, fingertips down, press water backward not down.
5. **Kick** — small, fast, from the hips, ankles relaxed. Kick is for position, not propulsion.

### Drill vocabulary
Kick on side (6-kick switch) · Catch-up · Fingertip drag · Single-arm · Sculling · Zipper drill · Head-up polo swim (sighting practice)

### Non-negotiables
Get **one coached session or one video review** in Weeks 1–2. You cannot see your own stroke, and 30 minutes of external feedback will outperform 6 weeks of self-directed practice. Use fins for drills only. Avoid the pull buoy as a crutch — it hides exactly the body-position fault you're trying to fix.

## 5B. Advanced: Threshold & Speed Development

**Who it's for:** established stroke, swims 2500 m+ per session, CSS around 1:30–1:50/100 m.
**Goal by Week 12:** drop 4–8 s/100 m off CSS, and hold pace in a 1500–3000 m effort.
**Frequency:** 4–5 swims/wk, 3000–4500 m per session, 14–20 km/wk.

### Session types

| Type | Prescription | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Threshold (CSS)** | 10–20 × 100 @ CSS; 6–8 × 200 @ CSS+2 | :10–:15 |
| **VO₂ / speed** | 16–20 × 50 @ CSS−6 s; 8 × 100 @ CSS−4 | :20–:30 |
| **Overspeed / neuromuscular** | 12–16 × 25 max, full recovery | :45 |
| **Aerobic endurance** | 3 × 800, or 1 × 3000 broken 400s | :30 |
| **Technique / recovery** | Drills, pull, paddles, sculling | — |

### Equipment protocol
- **Paddles:** max 20% of session volume. Bigger is not better — shoulder injuries live here.
- **Pull buoy + band:** builds body position and forces the catch to work. Excellent in moderation.
- **Snorkel:** the single most useful tool for fixing head position and catch without the breathing variable.
- **Fins:** ankle mobility and speed sensation. Not for building fitness.

### Open water conversion (if relevant)
From Week 6, add sighting every 6–8 strokes to one set per week, practice bilateral breathing in chop, and do at least three open-water sessions before any open-water event. Pool fitness does not transfer automatically — no walls, no line, no lane rope.
