# PART 13 — COMBAT SPORTS CONDITIONING

## What this trains that nothing else does

**Producing force while fatigued, against an unpredictable resistance.** Every other modality in this library is self-paced or externally fixed. Fighting is neither. The physiological demand is repeated 3–5 minute maximal-intermittent efforts on incomplete recovery — a heavy aerobic base supporting repeated alactic bursts, with the aerobic system doing most of the between-burst recovery work.

**The corollary most people get wrong:** conditioning for combat is *aerobically driven*. Athletes who only train "hard rounds" build a big anaerobic engine on a small aerobic one, and gas in round three. Base first, always.

**Note on integration:** if you're building toward a striking/discipleship track, this module is the conditioning half. Skill work — technique, timing, sparring — sits alongside it, not inside it. This program assumes 2–4 skill sessions per week happening independently and is designed *not* to bury them.

## 13A. Beginner: Fight-Shape Base

**Who it's for:** training a striking or grappling art 2–3×/wk, gassing early, no structured conditioning.
**Goal by Week 12:** hold 5 × 3-min rounds at working pace without pace collapse; build the neck, grip, and core that combat training demands.
**Frequency:** 3 conditioning sessions/wk alongside skill training.

### Session A — Aerobic base (Z2)
30 → 55 min continuous: roadwork, bike, or long-round shadowboxing at conversational effort. Grows 5 min/week to a 55-min ceiling. **This is the session people skip and the one that decides round three.**

### Session B — Round-based intervals

| Wks | Format |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | 5 × 2 min bag/shadow @ 70% effort, 1 min rest |
| **4** | 3 × 2 min easy — **deload** |
| 5–7 | 5 × 3 min @ 75–80%, 1 min rest; last 30 s of each round at high output |
| **8** | 3 × 3 min easy — **deload** |
| 9–11 | 6 × 3 min @ 80–85%, 45 s rest; alternate bag round / bodyweight-circuit round |
| 12 | **TEST: 5 × 3 min max-output rounds, 1 min rest** — score total strikes or reps per round; the drop-off from round 1 to round 5 is your number |

### Session C — Strength & armor (full body)
Trap bar deadlift 3 × 5 · Push press 3 × 5 · Chin-up 3 × AMRAP · Split squat 3 × 8/side · **Neck:** 4-way isometric harness or manual, 3 × 20 s each direction · **Grip:** towel hang 3 × max, plate pinch 3 × 30 s · Hollow hold + side plank 3 × 30 s.

### Non-negotiables at this level
- **Neck training is injury insurance, not vanity.** Stronger necks reduce head acceleration in impact, and the research on this is not ambiguous.
- **Hard sparring is a hard session.** Count it against your weekly hard-day budget (max 2–3). Nobody's conditioning program survives five hard spars a week.
- **Do not condition to failure the day before skill work.** Bad technique reps under fatigue become permanent.

## 13B. Advanced: Fight Camp Conditioning

**Who it's for:** competing or preparing for a specific event at Week 12.
**Goal by Week 12:** peak repeatability — hold output across 5 × 5 min rounds with minimal decrement.
**Frequency:** 5–6 conditioning sessions across 9–12 total weekly sessions with skill work.

### Block structure

| Wks | Block | Emphasis | Sparring load |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | **Aerobic base + strength** | 3 × Z2 (45–70 min), 2 × heavy lifting @ 80–87.5%, low-intensity technical rounds | Light/technical only |
| **4** | Deload | Z2 only, halved volume | Off |
| 5–7 | **Threshold + power endurance** | 4 × 4 min @ threshold; 10 × 1 min hard / 1 min easy; explosive lifting (jumps, throws, cleans) 5 × 3 | 1 hard spar/wk |
| **8** | Deload | | Off |
| 9–11 | **Specificity** | Round simulation at competition format; 30:30s; repeat-sprint ability; alactic power 8 × 10 s max, 90 s rest | 2 hard spars/wk, tapering after Wk 10 |
| 12 | **Taper** | Volume −60%, sharpness retained: short bursts, pad work, movement | None |

### The signature sessions

**Repeat-sprint ability (RSA):** 3 sets of 6 × 15 s max / 45 s easy, 4 min between sets. Bike or assault bike (lower injury cost than sprinting). This is the closest lab proxy to a scramble or an exchange.

**30:30s:** 20 × 30 s @ ~120% aerobic power / 30 s easy. The most efficient aerobic-power session in existence for fighters.

**Round simulation:** exact competition format (e.g. 5 × 5 min, 1 min rest) alternating 30 s max output / 60 s working pace within each round. Score output every round. **Round-to-round decrement under 10% is competition-ready.**

**Alactic power:** 8–10 × 8–10 s all-out with 90 s–2 min full recovery. Builds the explosive burst without the acidosis that ruins technique.

### Weight-cut caution
If a weight cut is part of the event, the last 2 weeks of this program are compromised by definition — plan for it. Cut water, not food, and not until the final 3–5 days. Chronic under-eating through Weeks 5–11 is the most common reason a camp produces a worse athlete than it started with. **Losing more than 1% of bodyweight per week during Weeks 1–8 means you're eating a conditioning program's worth of stress into a deficit and getting neither result.**

### Recovery under combat load
Sparring is the highest-cost session that exists — it carries mechanical trauma, neural fatigue, and (in striking) sub-concussive load. Schedule at least 48 h between hard spars, never spar hard within 24 h of a max-effort conditioning session, and treat any head impact with symptoms as a full stop, not a tough-it-out moment.
