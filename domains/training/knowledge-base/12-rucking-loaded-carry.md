# PART 12 — RUCKING & LOADED CARRY

## Why it earns a spot

Rucking is the highest-carryover, lowest-skill conditioning modality that exists. It builds aerobic capacity at Z2 intensity while simultaneously loading the spine, hips, and feet in a way that walking never does — and unlike running, the impact forces stay low while the time-under-load goes way up. It is also the only entry here you can do while carrying groceries, a toddler, or a job.

## The load rules (violating these is how people get hurt)

1. **Start at 10% of bodyweight**, cap early progress at 20%, work toward 30% only after Week 8.
2. **Increase load by no more than 5 lb every 2 weeks**, or distance by no more than 1 mile per week — **never both in the same week.**
3. **Pace standard:** 15:00/mi is the military benchmark. 20:00/mi is a comfortable start. Get the distance first, then the pace, then the load.
4. **Load rides high and tight** — the weight should sit between the shoulder blades, not sagging at the lumbar spine. A sagging ruck is a back injury on a timer.
5. **Feet are the failure point, not lungs.** Broken-in boots or trail runners, wool socks, and blister prevention (tape hot spots *before* they blister) determine whether you finish.

## Posture under load
Slight forward lean from the ankles, not the waist. Short, quick steps. Eyes up. **Do not run under heavy load in the first 8 weeks** — the ground reaction forces multiply and the Achilles and shins take it first.

## 12A. Beginner: Building the Ruck Base

**Who it's for:** anyone who can walk 3 miles comfortably.
**Goal by Week 12:** 12 miles at 30 lb under 4 hours, or 6 miles at 30 lb under 90 min.
**Frequency:** 2 rucks/wk + 2 strength sessions + 1 unloaded walk/hike.

| Wk | Ruck 1 (short/faster) | Ruck 2 (long) | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 mi @ 18:00/mi | 3 mi easy | 20 lb |
| 2 | 2 mi @ 17:30 | 4 mi easy | 20 lb |
| 3 | 3 mi @ 17:00 | 5 mi easy | 20 lb |
| **4** | 2 mi easy | 3 mi easy | 15 lb — **deload** |
| 5 | 3 mi @ 16:30 | 6 mi easy | 25 lb |
| 6 | 3 mi @ 16:00 | 7 mi easy | 25 lb |
| 7 | 4 mi @ 16:00 | 8 mi easy | 25 lb |
| **8** | 2 mi easy | 4 mi easy | 20 lb — **deload** |
| 9 | 4 mi @ 15:30 | 9 mi easy | 30 lb |
| 10 | 4 mi @ 15:00 + hills | 10 mi easy | 30 lb |
| 11 | 3 mi @ 15:00 | **12 mi** | 30 lb |
| 12 | 2 mi easy | **TEST: 6 mi @ 30 lb for time** | 30 lb |

### Supporting strength (2×/wk)
Trap bar deadlift 3 × 5 · Rear-foot elevated split squat 3 × 8/side · Farmer carry 4 × 60 yd (heavy) · Single-leg calf raise 3 × 15 · Suitcase carry 3 × 40 yd/side · Bent-over row 3 × 10 · Dead bug 3 × 10/side.

### Carry variations worth rotating in
| Carry | Load | Trains |
|---|---|---|
| Farmer | 50–100% BW total | Grip, traps, posture |
| Suitcase (one side) | 25–40% BW | Lateral core, anti-lateral flexion |
| Front rack | 25–50% BW | Upper back, bracing, breathing under load |
| Overhead | 15–30% BW | Shoulder stability, thoracic extension |
| Sandbag bear hug | 30–60% BW | Awkward-object capacity, breathing restriction |
| Yoke / zercher walk | 75–150% BW | Whole-body rigidity |

## 12B. Advanced: Heavy Ruck / Selection Prep

**Who it's for:** already rucks 12 miles at 35 lb; preparing for a heavy event, selection, or a long mountain objective.
**Goal by Week 12:** 12 miles @ 45 lb under 3 hours, plus 20+ mile capability at 35 lb.
**Frequency:** 3 rucks + 3 strength/conditioning, 8–12 h/wk.

| Wks | Short/speed ruck | Mid ruck | Long ruck | Load |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 4 mi @ 14:30 | 6–8 mi hills | 10 → 14 mi | 35 lb |
| **4** | 3 mi easy | — | 8 mi | 25 lb — **deload** |
| 5–7 | 5 mi @ 14:00 | 8–10 mi hills | 15 → 18 mi | 40 lb |
| **8** | 3 mi easy | — | 10 mi | 30 lb — **deload** |
| 9–11 | 6 mi @ 13:30 | 10–12 mi w/ vert | 20 → 22 mi | 45 lb |
| 12 | 3 mi primer | — | **TEST: 12 mi @ 45 lb for time** | 45 lb |

### Advanced session types
- **Ruck intervals:** 8 × 400 m ruck-run @ 35 lb, 90 s walk recovery. Introduce only from Week 5, and only after 8 weeks of injury-free loaded walking.
- **Vertical ruck:** 2,500–4,000 ft of gain at 35–45 lb. The single best preparation for mountain work and the closest thing to selection-day reality.
- **Ruck + work:** 3 mi ruck → 50 sandbag getups → 3 mi ruck. Trains the actual demand, which is never just walking.
- **Sandbag medleys:** 5 rounds of 100 yd bear-hug carry (80–150 lb) + 10 shoulder-to-shoulder + 20 yd drag.

### Managing the risk
Rucking's injuries are cumulative and quiet: stress reactions in the tibia and metatarsals, plantar fascia, and lumbar irritation. **Any bone-point tenderness you can cover with one fingertip means stop and unload for 10 days.** Total weekly loaded mileage should climb no faster than 10%, and heavy load (40+ lb) should appear on at most one session per week.
