# PART 8 — THE OTHER CATEGORIES WORTH ADDING

You asked what else is pertinent. Four, in order of how much I'd argue for them:

### 8.1 Mobility, Movement Quality & Tissue Resilience — *the one I'd insist on*
Not a category alongside the others so much as **a layer under all seven.** Every program above has a joint that fails first: runners' ankles and hips, lifters' thoracic spine and shoulders, swimmers' shoulders, cyclists' hip flexors and neck, calisthenics athletes' wrists and elbows.

**Minimum effective dose:** 10 min post-session on the day's joints + 1 × 30 min dedicated session weekly. **Loaded end-range work** (Jefferson curls, ATG split squats, Copenhagen planks, tibialis raises, controlled articular rotations) — not passive static stretching, which does very little for injury risk.

### 8.2 Rowing / Erg & Loaded Carries (Rucking)
Full-body, high-aerobic, near-zero impact. The best running substitute for people whose knees or shins are the limiter, the best conditioning modality for lifters, and — in rucking's case — the highest-carryover, lowest-skill way to build real-world work capacity. A 12-week ruck build (30 lb / 3 mi → 45 lb / 12 mi) is a legitimate standalone program.

### 8.3 Combat Sports Conditioning
Distinct energy-system demands (repeated 3–5 min max-effort rounds with incomplete recovery), plus grip, neck, and clinch-specific strength. Trains a quality none of the seven above trains: **producing force while fatigued, against an unpredictable resistance.** Given your Shaolin discipleship track, the striking-endurance and conditioning side of this belongs in your library — I'd build it as its own 12-week module.

### 8.4 Recovery & Nervous-System Programming
Sleep architecture, HRV-guided autoregulation, heat/cold protocols, breathwork, and deload discipline. Everyone treats this as an afterthought; at high training loads it's the actual limiter. This one pairs directly with your nervous-system agent work rather than living as a training module.

**Two more, if you want completeness:** *Olympic weightlifting* (snatch/clean & jerk — technically distinct enough from powerlifting to warrant its own module) and *strongman / odd-object* training (carries, stones, yokes — the best bridge between powerlifting and hybrid).
