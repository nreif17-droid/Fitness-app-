# PART 17 — EVIDENCE UPDATES & THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE PRESCRIPTIONS

Volumes I and II state prescriptions. This file states **why**, and flags where recent
evidence has sharpened or challenged them. Its job is to let the AI coach explain and
adapt intelligently rather than only recite tables — and to record where the library's
existing numbers are well-supported vs. conventional.

---

## Hypertrophy: the dose-response, updated

A 2025 meta-regression (67 studies, 2,058 participants) is the best current picture of
how volume and frequency drive hypertrophy and strength.

**Volume:**
- **Posterior probability of a positive effect on both hypertrophy and strength: 100%.**
  More volume produces more growth and more strength.
- **But with diminishing returns**, and the diminishing returns are **considerably more
  pronounced for strength** than for hypertrophy.
- At least **10 sets per muscle per week** outperformed fewer than 10 for muscle mass.

**Frequency:**
- For **hypertrophy**, the effect of frequency was **compatible with negligible** —
  posterior probability under 100%. Total volume matters; how you split it matters much
  less.
- For **strength**, frequency's effect was **100% positive** with diminishing returns.
  Strength is a skill; more practice sessions help.

### What this means for the library

- **`02-bodybuilding.md`'s 10–20 sets/muscle/week band is well-supported.** No change.
- **The "2× per week beats 1× at equal volume" claim in the bodybuilding file is
  overstated for hypertrophy specifically.** The current best evidence says frequency is
  largely a tool for distributing volume tolerably, not an independent growth driver.
  For *strength*, the frequency claim holds. The coach should not promise hypertrophy
  gains from splitting the same volume across more days.
- **The MEV/MAV/MRV volume-landmark framework remains a useful practical scaffold**,
  but should be presented as a programming heuristic, not as precisely measured
  physiological thresholds — the underlying dose-response is smooth and individual,
  not stepped.

## Proximity to failure

The library's "0–4 RIR counts as a stimulating set; 6+ RIR is mostly junk volume" is a
reasonable practical rule. Nuance worth carrying:

- Sets closer to failure produce more stimulus **per set**, but cost disproportionately
  more fatigue — which reduces the total volume you can recover from.
- This is why the 12-week arc in every program **starts at 3–4 RIR and progresses to
  1–2 RIR**, rather than training to failure year-round. That structure is doing real
  work, not being cautious for its own sake.
- Training to failure on compound lifts is the worst stimulus-to-fatigue trade in the
  library. The advanced bodybuilding program's "last set to failure" in the overreach
  block is deliberately narrow — it is not a general recommendation.

## Where the library is conventional rather than strongly evidenced

Intellectual honesty about the knowledge base's own confidence levels. The coach should
hold these more loosely:

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| 12-week block length | **Convention.** A practical planning unit, not a physiologically special duration. Adaptations don't respect quarters. |
| Week 4 / 8 deload timing | **Reasonable convention.** The *principle* (planned recovery before it's forced) is well-supported; the specific calendar is arbitrary and the library already says autoregulation overrides it. |
| Specific percentage tables in peaking blocks | **Well-established practice**, thinner controlled evidence. The library's own rule — "when the % and the RPE disagree, obey the RPE" — is the right instinct. |
| 80/20 polarized endurance distribution | **Strong** observational and interventional support. |
| The 10% weekly mileage rule | **Weak as a precise rule**, useful as a brake. The underlying principle (connective tissue adapts slower than the aerobic system) is sound; the specific number is folklore that happens to be conservative enough to help. |
| Zone models (5-zone, %HRmax) | **Useful abstraction.** Zone boundaries are individual; %HRmax formulas carry large error. Prefer effort/talk test and threshold-anchored zones over age-predicted HR. |
| Mobility: 1–3°/week of real end-range change | **Plausible, weakly evidenced.** The direction (loaded end-range beats passive stretching) is well-supported. |

**Why this table exists:** a coach that presents everything with equal certainty is
wrong in a way users eventually detect. Being honest about confidence is a
differentiator, not a weakness.
